The directive governing the energy performance of buildings was recast, and the change most owners noticed was the headline about zero-emission new construction. For an industrial owner in Romania, that is the least relevant part of it. Almost nothing in your portfolio is new, and almost everything in it is now in scope of something it was not in scope of before.

## The centre of gravity moved to existing stock

The earlier framework treated existing buildings mainly through the renovation trigger: if you carried out a major renovation, requirements attached to it. If you did not renovate, very little happened.

The recast approach is different in kind. It sets trajectories for the existing stock as a whole, identifies the worst-performing segment of it specifically, and attaches obligations to that segment on a timetable rather than on an event. The practical effect is that doing nothing is no longer a way of staying outside the regime. It is a way of arriving at the obligation later, with less time.

## What this means in sequence

For an owner with several industrial buildings, three things follow, and the order matters more than the list.

**First, establish where each building actually sits.** Not where its certificate says it sits. Energy performance certificates on industrial stock in this region are frequently derived from assumed values, produced quickly for a transaction, and wrong in both directions. We have seen certificates understate consumption by thirty per cent and overstate it by nearly as much.

The remedy is metered data. A year of billing data and two to four weeks of sub-metering will tell you where the energy actually goes, and that distribution is almost never the one the certificate implies.

**Second, find out which buildings are in the worst-performing segment.** The obligations bite hardest and earliest there. A portfolio owner who knows which two of their eleven buildings are in that band has a different and much cheaper problem than one who treats all eleven as equally urgent.

**Third, sequence the measures rather than listing them.** This is where most roadmaps fail. The measures interact, and they interact in ways that change what should be bought.

## The interaction problem

The saving from improving a building envelope and the saving from replacing its heat source are not additive. Improve the envelope first and the heat demand falls, which means the replacement plant should be smaller, cheaper, and in some cases a different technology entirely. Replace the plant first and you have bought capacity you are about to make redundant, and you will keep paying for it for fifteen years.

This sounds obvious written down. It is routinely got wrong, because plant replacement is capital expenditure with a clear invoice and a clear payback, while envelope work is disruptive, harder to price, and easier to defer.

The sequencing question is therefore not only technical. It is about which measure is easy to approve, and a roadmap that ignores that is a roadmap that will not be followed.

## Where the ventilation requirements catch people

The recast directive strengthened indoor environmental quality provisions alongside the energy ones, and these are the requirements most often missed in industrial buildings, because the building is thought of as a process space rather than an occupied one.

If a deep retrofit tightens the envelope of a building with offices, a control room or a quality laboratory inside it, the ventilation provision for those spaces has to be reconsidered as part of the same project. Discovering this after the envelope work is complete means opening it again.

## What to do this year

Meter. Everything else on the list depends on knowing where the energy goes, and a year of data cannot be acquired retrospectively.

An owner who starts metering now has a calibrated baseline when the obligations arrive, which is also the baseline needed to demonstrate a saving afterwards. An owner who waits will be asked to demonstrate improvement against a baseline nobody measured.

## A note on what is not yet settled

Member state transposition is still in progress, and the thresholds that define the worst-performing segment, the timetable attached to it, and the exemptions available to industrial buildings are all set nationally within the directive's framework.

We are not going to pretend the detail is fixed. What is fixed is the direction, and the measurement that every version of the detail will require. That is why the recommendation above is to meter rather than to retrofit: metering is useful under every version of the rules, and a retrofit chosen before the rules settle may be the wrong retrofit.
