Industrial structures built before the current Eurocode fatigue provisions frequently carry connection details that nobody would specify today. Cover plates welded across a flange, intermittent fillet welds at a bracket, a runway beam spliced where the bending moment is highest. The temptation, on first inspection, is to classify the detail, look up its category, and conclude that the structure is deficient.

That conclusion is usually premature, and it is expensive to act on.

## The question is remaining life, not adequacy

A fatigue assessment of an existing structure answers a different question from a fatigue check in design. In design the question is whether a detail will survive a specified life under an assumed spectrum. In assessment, both the life so far and the spectrum are facts rather than assumptions, and they can be measured.

The useful question is whether the remaining life of the detail is shorter than the remaining life of the plant it serves. A runway beam with twelve years of fatigue life left is not a problem in a hall scheduled for demolition in eight. The same beam is a serious problem if the owner has just bought a crane with a twenty-five year programme.

## Three inputs, in order of how much they move the answer

**The duty spectrum.** This is almost always the input that dominates, and it is almost always assumed rather than measured. Crane classifications in particular are routinely applied one or two classes more severely than the actual duty, because the classification was chosen when the line was designed and the line has since slowed, changed product, or moved to fewer, heavier lifts.

Instrumenting a crane for two weeks and recording the actual load spectrum is inexpensive relative to the steelwork that the assumption can add to a strengthening scope. In our experience the recorded spectrum is lighter than the assumed one roughly four times in five, and when it is heavier, that is worth knowing urgently.

**The number of cycles already accumulated.** Production records, where they exist, are better than an estimate from the building's age. Where they do not exist, maintenance records, motor run-hours and even energy metering can bound the figure. A bound is worth considerably more than a round number chosen for conservatism.

**The detail category itself.** This matters, but less than the two above, because it enters the calculation as a coefficient while the stress range enters as a cube. A one-category error changes the answer by a factor of roughly 1.6. A twenty per cent error in the stress range changes it by about 1.7, and stress range errors of twenty per cent are easy to make from assumed geometry.

## What to look at on site

Fatigue cracks initiate where the stress concentration is, which is frequently not where the eye is drawn. On crane runways the bracket-to-column weld toe produces more cracks than the beam splice that looks more alarming. On gantries it is the attachment of secondary members to the main chord.

Magnetic particle inspection at weld toes on the details the analysis identifies as governing is a far better use of a day on site than a general visual survey of everything. Let the analysis direct the inspection, then let the inspection correct the analysis.

Check also for repairs. A detail that has been repaired has a different category from the one shown on the drawing, and a repair carried out without a procedure may be worse than the original. We have twice found weld repairs that had introduced a notch more severe than the crack they closed.

## Reporting a fatigue assessment

A fatigue assessment should not end in a verdict. It should end in an inspection interval.

The honest output is a remaining life figure per governing detail, the assumptions that figure rests on, and the interval at which the detail should be reinspected to confirm that the assumptions still hold. A structure that is told to be inspected every three years is managed. A structure that is declared adequate is forgotten until something cracks.

Where the assessment relies on a measured duty spectrum, say what would invalidate it: a product change, a rate increase, a different crane. Those are the events the owner can actually watch for, and they are more useful in a report than a safety factor the owner cannot interpret.

## When strengthening is genuinely the answer

Sometimes it is. A detail with a category below 40 carrying a significant stress range in a structure with decades of intended life will not be argued out of strengthening by better data.

Even then the measurement pays, because it establishes which details need work and which merely look similar. The difference between strengthening eleven connections and strengthening forty-four is almost never structural behaviour. It is the quality of the information the assessment was built on.
